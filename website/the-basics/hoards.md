## Hoards Overview

Hoards are particularly challenging encounters that yield magic items, some of which are unavailable in shops. You can challenge a hoard at any level, even if you are a higher or lower tier than the intended tier. Before you start, there’s a few things to keep in mind when it comes to hoards.

**Hoar****ds must be ran in the direct channel/thread**

For tracking purposes, hoards should be run in the hoard threads, **including if you are soloing**. This is to make it easier on staff to monitor hoard runs and ensure everyone is doing it correctly.

**Hoard Rewards**

Hoards can only be won by defeating all monsters by downing them, aka reducing them to 0 hp. You cannot win hoard rewards from monsters who have fled or by casting spells such as Banishment or Force Cage. Even if some monsters remain, you forfeit all loot if a monster flees.

**Hoard Cool-Down**

You cannot complete the same hoard twice in a 2 week period. Please set a cool down time. Many players use the /reminder function. You cannot-reattempt a hoard you fled from or lost until the end of the 2 week cool-down.

**Hoard Distance**

You cannot move more than 120 ft away from the closest hoard monster or else you have “fled” combat, unless a hoard details a different distance restriction or exception.

**Pre-Buffing**

You cannot cast spells, drink potions, or other pre-buffs before a hoard once you arrive in its location. The only exception to this is healing potions and using healing abilities in between hoards in one location, as well as at-will abilities/at-will buffs/at-will spells that take less than five minutes to use/cast. If it takes more than five minutes, this must be done during the one allotted short rest per hoard.

Other buffs may be cast prior to arriving in a location, but remember, it takes an hour to travel to a hoard.

**Stealth**

You cannot attempt to stealth at the start of hoards to gain surprise. You may still attempt to hide in later rounds (I.e. Rogue’s Cunning Action).

**Loot Generation (and Need vs Greed)**

After calculating the gold rewards using the **!calc** command, you can roll for the Hoard Loot. Only one player can roll for the loot. Run the command **!lootgen <hoard tier #>**.

1. **Declaring Need:**

- Before rolling !r 1d100 for selection order, players may declare they "Need" **ONE** item from the loot list.
- A player may only use the "Need" option once per hoard. **NOT ONCE PER TIER -- ONCE PER HOARD.**

1. **Resolving Need Conflicts:**

- If two or more players declare "Need" for the same item, those players will roll !r 1d100 to determine who gets the item.
- The highest roll wins the item.

1. **Post-Need Rolls:**

- Once "Need" items are resolved, all players roll !r 1d100 to determine the selection order for the remaining loot.
- Players who declared a "Need" and received their item will skip their turn in the first round of the regular loot-picking phase.

1. **Moderator Oversight:**

- If a player declares "Need" for an item without a valid reason, a @Moderator may be called to determine if the declaration is justified.

**Key Notes:**

- "Need" should only be declared for items that are critically important to a player's build, role, or immediate goals.
- Misusing the "Need" option undermines the system's fairness and may result in penalties decided by the Moderator.
- Greed rolls and loot picking follow the original system after all "Need" items are settled:
- Those who wish to loot must roll !r 1d100 to determine the selection order. Then, select ONE item per turn, with the highest roll going first. Repeat until all items are distributed or everyone passes. The remainder is sold and split evenly among the party.

**Peeking**

As a general rule, you cannot ‘peek’ at encounters or hoards to see what you are up against and decide afterwards if you would like to do them. The only exception to this is hoards which have multiple tiered stages. If you are doing a hoard run in a location like this (i.e. Rite of Primordial Echoes) you may peek only once and must declare before you roll the alias that you are peeking.

**Resting Between Hoards**

When doing hoards with multiple tiers you may only take 1 short rest per 'run'. Choose when to rest wisely. During the rest, you may only recover abilities, spend hit dice, swap a single attunement, heal, or drink healing potions.

In between hoards, you only have five minutes until the next one unless the hoard text states otherwise (Catacombs, for example, has a few tiers that take a few hours in between each tier). You only have time to heal and use at-will abilities/at-will buffs/at-will spells that take less than five minutes to use/cast. If it takes more than five minutes, this must be done during the one allotted short rest per hoard.

**Rolling a Hoard When You Don't Want to Do One**

While adventuring, should you roll your d20 encounter and a hoard shows up, you may choose to skip the hoard. You must make this decision before looking at the hoard. However, should you choose to skip the hoard, simply re-roll for the other encounter types and complete a regular encounter.

**Cheesing or** **Camping** **a Hoard**

Avoid camping or cheesing the monsters when using spells, abilities, or items like Resilient Sphere, Wall of Force, etc. If moderators review the combat and feel that the use of these spells was unfair, you may be asked to give up your rewards from the hoard. Examples:

- The entirety of the party hides in a Wall of Force to recover health and cast buffs on each other

## Hoards Legibility

Hoard combats must be written in such a way that a 3rd party (someone who was niether player nor DM) can read through the chat log and understand what was happening without the need of having it explained to them by a primary party (players and/or dm). Effective immediately hoard chat logs should contain both narrative descriptions of player character and non player character actions as well as any required rolls or removal of resources or health. If an error occurs a write up of the error and the steps taken to correct it must be included followed by any adjustment of resources and health required.

Include proper distances, use of reach, and movement speed used.

**WE REQUIRE THE USE OF THE** **!init note <name> <note information>** **FUNCTION TO KEEP DISTANCE!**

- I.e.: On his turn, Joe moves from the starting line (0ft) forward 30ft. To show this changes, Joe would use the command !init note Joe 30ft to mark his current position. On his next turn, Joe walks 20ft to the left, while staying 30ft ahead of the starting line, his new note would be !init note Joe 30ft, 20ft Left.

Use this same formatting when flying into the air, burrowing under ground, or swimming to help keep things clear! When it comes to taking notes, try to keep it brief when using the !init note command and explain in more detail with RP. That way you can always scroll back up if something is unclear.

**DMs:** Whoever is running the hoard, you are responsible for your own character's notes and the notes of the monsters in the combat.

**Players:** You are responsible for yourself and any minions, familiars, or companions you have with you.

Please keep OOC chatter to a minimum, and mention special abilities to increase clarity. (I.e. Joe attacks three times with his Dreaded Ambusher)

Hoards that are not legibly written will result in either a forfeiture of the loot, or a play by play discussion with a moderator. While all combat should be legible, the standard for hoards is even more so.

## Seriousness of Hoard Enemies

Hoard enemies are deadly, serious, and challenging monsters meant to give players that feeling of epic battles. Many of them are integral pieces of the lore of Nymrathis. As such, DMing players must play these monsters as serious and intelligent in both roleplay and combat.

This is integral to maintaining player immersion and the integrity of the world lore.

While there’s room for fun, silly encounter, these are to be limited to Dynamic Encounters only. Think of hoard monsters as the BBEGs to a campaign. And not a joke campaign.

Hoard Monsters should not be:

- Speaking in silly accents
- Playing into memes and jokes, beyond “dark humour” to frighten PCs
- Flirting with the PCs
- Otherwise acting in a way that does not respect their status as legendary, lore-defining creatures

Failure to do so may result in a forfeiture of DMing privileges.

## Locating Hoards

You can now find hoards in a specific location after partaking in **3 encounters in that location that involve combat**. The exception to this are the !dencs done to camp and long rest.

Once those 3 combat encounters are finished, one of the player characters may roll a **DC 17 Investigation** check. On a success you find the hoard location and may proceed to it. You don't find the hoard on a failure, however, you may roll this check again after each subsequent combat encounter.

## Dungeons

Dungeons are extremely challenging encounters that are essentially multileveled Hoards. These Dungeons will give you the chance to gain several Hoard rewards, but pose a far more serious threat. **Players are advised to bring full parties and be prepared for extensive combat and great danger.** Dungeons will have their own unique instructions for when you can rest, when you gain rewards, and any special rules to follow, so please make sure you are reading the Dungeon descriptions very closely!
