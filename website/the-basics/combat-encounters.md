## Introduction

This is likely the most complicated aspect of rules on this server due to three unique factors:

1) Theory of Mind Combat (Maps can be used but are use sparingly here)

2) Player Run Combat (DMs run some quests and encounters, but day to day is peer run)

3) Texted Based, Channel to Channel Travel

These rules around combat and encounters exist to ensure that we get as close to a mechanically balanced game as possible: Your loot is earned, the combat is fun, and the monsters aren't pushovers. Please ensure to follow the rules and guidelines laid out below.

### Party Size and Tiers

There are four tiers of play:

- Tier 1 | 1-4
- Tier 2 | 5-10
- Tier 3 | 11-16
- Tier 4 | 17-20

Adventurers should do their best to stick to adventuring in tiers consisting of similar leveled characters. A level 5 and a level 16 adventuring together is risky.

**The maximum party size is 6**, regardless of the make-up. Encounters are balanced for a group of 4-5 adventurers in the middle of their tier. The balance of hoards varies to some degree, but most are designed to be a challenge for a group of 4-6 at the top of their tier with some magic items.

### Party Size Bonuses

You now get certain buffs based on the size of your party. This is to help incentivize different styles of play. Each party size comes with its own initiative snippet!

**!i join #man** with # replaced by the size of your party (1-6).

In the case of Party Size Bonuses, party size only counts Player Characters. ❌ No Companions! ❌

Some bonuses can't be automated (bonuses to specific checks and saves), so you'll just have to keep them in mind!

### Party Formations

Before journeying out, players can declare a **Party Formation.** As long as one player remains at the starting position (0ft) other players can declare themselves starting behind that player up to 30 feet. This includes flying or swimming where plausible. Burrowing is not allowed, as it would be very hard to travel as a group with a member beneath the ground.

Enemies will always start in front of players. The only exception to this is when enemies roll stealth and all succeed as hiding from the party. In this situation, the enemies will ambush the party from the front or from behind, depending on which option is more tactically efficient. Usually this will be from behind to pick off separated party members.

A Party Formation can only be changed in safe areas (non combat zones like cities) or by short resting in the wilds for an hour.

### Mixed Tier Adventures

Encounters are balanced with groups of 4-5 players in mind. The absolute max number of players in any adventuring group is 6. Adventuring groups can only span across two tiers.

- I.e. T1 and T2 can adventure together but T3 and T1 cannot.

### Pathing: Danger Zones

Combat areas are broken down into three levels of danger, which determine how your party will roll for encounters!  
  
🟢**Low Danger** - Parties must play at 1 Tier **BELOW** their highest Level party member or higher

**🟠Medium Danger** - Parties must play **AT THE TIER** of their highest Level party member or higher

**🔴High Danger** - Parties must play **AT THE TIER** of their highest Level party member or higher, with the understanding that the encounters in this area are balanced to be harder for players of that Tier.

In example:

- A group of four T2 adventurers can do T1 dencs in any Low Danger zones, but must fight T2 dencs in any Medium or High Danger zones.
- A group consisting of two T3s and one T4 can do T3 dencs in any Low Danger zones, but must fight T4 dencs in any Medium or High Danger zones.

**Summons over 1 hour, minions, and pets count towards the part make up.** The only exceptions to this are familiars and class specific companions I.e. rangers companions, drakewarden drake, steel defender, artillerist, ect.

## Combat Basics

Encounters are largely run using **!denc** commands. Most encounters are geared towards the standard leveling tiers (1-4) Special Dynamic Encounters may use **!denc <location> <tier>** formula differently where <tier #> does not reflect the tier it is intended for. These will be defined in the location description.

Because these encounters are randomly generated, and much of the combat is player run, the following rules ensure the monsters are acting appropriately and players are not acting in bad-faith to give themselves or their friends an upper hand.

### Encounter Basics

Creatures should use their abilities. Creatures with stealth proficiency will always attempt to ambush the party. Creatures with powerful recharge abilities should use them as soon as possible, should it tactically make sense. Try your best to run monsters true to their stat blocks. Some variation may be fun for good storytelling, but ignoring key aspects of the monster's abilities, lore, or behavior to get a ‘leg up’ is not permitted.

Some creatures may flee, fight, persuade, deceive, or turn around and walk away if you're too far from them to catch up-and they're smart enough to realize this. If a creature is consistently stuck at the edge of combat, they can turn and use their speed & action to run away.

Monster in both The Wilds and The Arena will operate in environments that are favorable to them. For example, a drow would never conduct a surface raid in broad daylight with no shade, and a shark would never be willingly beached. Disadvantages like Sunlight Sensitivity can be easily countered by a thick canopy of trees or a shadow cast by a large mountain.  
  
**Pre-Buffing**

We do not allow the use of "pre-buffing" with Guidance when it comes to Stealth, Perception, or Initiative in the wilds. This is simply to help with the balance of encounters and the ease of gameplay.

**Distance and Fleeing**

Encounters start at **1d10*10** feet away. You may use the **!dist** command to calculate this. To flee, one has to reach over 120 ft from the closest monster. If you choose to flee you must return all the way back to the city and cannot make a "u-turn" and proceed back forwards in the wilds until you make it back to Nymrathis. You may fight beyond the 120 ft mark, unless you are in a hoard, the arena, or a special environment that states otherwise. However, if you are alone, the monsters will flee as detailed below (these rules do not apply to hoard monsters).

If a monster is over 120 ft from the closest party member and cannot reach one within its turn or attack it will choose to flee, forfeiting all rewards the party might've gotten from defeating it. If this is in a hoard the party also will lose all rewards related to that hoard. Creatures may also flee if they are intelligent enough to realize an attack is futile, such as if a PC is 'camping' behind a Wall of Force, Instant Fortress, or similar 'bubble'.

**Alignment & Behavior**

Monster without alignments can be tricky to run, so it's best to leave these up to chance. For beasts that are not obviously docile or aggressive hunters, you can roll for their state of mind. Are they territorial? Hungry? Injured? Relaxing? Scared?

For monster that are "any alignment" you must use the **!alignment** alias to determine either their alignment or how hostile they may be to you. Monsters that are "typically" one alignment may still be rolled for **!alignment** as well, or simply played as their typical alignment.

**Travel Time**

Travel between each area in the wilds takes 1 hour, but the travel time between encounters within the same wilderness channel is only 20 minutes. This means that hour long spells will last you three encounters as long as it is in the same channel. Please keep this in mind when holding concentration on spells such as Hex or Hunter's Mark. Remember: Short rests also take 1 hour.

**Light and Environment**

PCs may choose to venture out into the wilderness at specific times, such as only venturing out at night even if the server time is not currently set as nighttime. The server time is only for RP within the city.

Use the Server Clock at the start of your adventure to determine the lighting of your travel encounters and outside hoards. Additionally, a group can decide unanimously to travel at a different time than the clock, which must be stated at the start of the adventure. You do not need to keep track of time that passes; whatever the light condition is when you start your journey is the lighting that will persist all the way through. After a long rest, a new travel time can be selected.

Unless otherwise stated in the pinned location description, assume the lighting for all dencs is 6:00 - 17:59 Bright Light; 18:00 - 5:59 Darkness. Remember when traveling at night that creatures with no Darkvision will be carrying some form of light with them, and that during the day, creatures with sunlight sensitivity are assumed to be attacking in a place of Dim Light or Darkness. Monsters will always attack in the lighting environment that gives them the best advantage.

If players roll **!denc** and it is an environment/weather encounter (i.e. storm, blizzard, etc) They may choose to either roll a second **!denc** encounter which must be completed using the rules in the DMG for that environment or turn back.

## Flight, Burrow, Swim

If it makes sense to do so, creatures with Flight, Burrow, or Swim speeds should use them in combat and may start the encounter in the air, underground, or underwater.

To utilize your flight, burrow, or swim, at least 1 ally must be reachable by the enemies attacks. If no ally is present or reachable by the enemy, you can only take the following actions: Dash, Dodge, or Hide. You must use your movement to come within reach of the enemy and finish the encounter or move away from the enemies beyond 120ft to flee and return to the previous area.

You may use your bonus action as per usual, but you MUST use your full movement with the intention of engaging or fleeing from the enemies. Once you make this decision, you cannot change your mind, with the exception of fleeing by ground after you have enageged the enemy and found yourself unequipped to handle the combat. The enemy will wait for you to come into range if you choose to engage. They will chase you back to the previous area if you flee.

- Eg. Joe is in the air 70ft flying when his ally in melee with the enemy is killed. Joe decides he will Engage rather than Flee. On his turn, Joe uses his flight move speed to move closer to the enemy, and his action to hide. With his bonus action he drinks a potion of healing. Next turn he uses his movement once again to get back into melee with the enemy. The combat continues on the ground.
- Eg. Joe is burrowed 30ft under ground when his ally in melee with the enemy is killed. Joe decides he will Flee rather than Engage. On his turn, Joe uses his burrow move speed and his action to dash away from the enemy, repeating this action on each of his turns until crossing the 120ft mark. He has successfully fled the combat and returned to the previous area. If he was fighting in [ⓝ①🟢cerro-foothills](https://discord.com/channels/1239236397672697987/1240817693909717022) he would be sent back to [〇⓪🟢verdant-expanse](https://discord.com/channels/1239236397672697987/1240811987911311370) and on arrival would need to complete a new encounter. After that, Joe can continue heading back to the city or go forward to [ⓝ①🟢cerro-foothills](https://discord.com/channels/1239236397672697987/1240817693909717022) to continue his journey like normal.

Enemies, Players, and Companions must stay within 120ft of at least one opponent when using flight, burrow, or swim, regardless of the positioning of your allies and how close they are. This is an exception to the usual Distance and Fleeing rule specifically for these types of movement.

## Sneak, Perception, and Surprise

Creatures with a stealth proficiency will always attempt to ambush the players, regardless of the starting distance, and stealth proficient creatures will attempt their stealth first. Only after the creatures attempt to stealth, all the PCs perceive all the enemies, and the starting distance is 60 ft or over can the PCs counter with their own stealth checks. Stealth is complicated, so please read the next section carefully...

Players may attempt to sneak and utilize the 'surprise' condition to their advantage, but keep in mind this means enemies may do the same. A creature is considered surprised if they fail to spot a single enemy. If a creature is surprised, they can’t move or take an action on their first turn of the combat, and they can’t take a reaction until that turn ends. A member of a group can be surprised even if the other members aren’t.

After rolling for a dynamic encounter, players may attempt to hide or sneak if there is at least 60 feet between them and the enemy (Guidance is not applicable for either the perception or stealth check unless one subtle spells it). This 60 ft distance minimum restriction is not in place after the encounter starts, where one can follow the regular stealth rules. Please use common sense. Unless you are unseen, you cannot stealth while right next to an enemy. Players must beat the enemy's active perception checks and their passive perception in order to be 'hidden' and 'surprise'.

Keep in mind, enemies may also do the same, though they are allowed to stealth from any starting distance instead of 60 minimum.

You cannot stealth at the start of a hoard encounter (detailed below).

FAQ:

Does getting damaged or hit break stealth? No.

Do abilities such as guiding bolt break stealth on the target? No, unless they were sneaking via darkness, which is usually not the case, as they would be using cover instead.

**Perception Checks, How Do They Work?**

For both a player or enemy, in the case of a perception roll lower than their passive perception, they will use their passive perception. Functionally, this means one’s passive perception is the minimum value for their perception. One’s passive perception is equal to 10 + their perception modifier, although advantage and disadvantage adds or subtracts 5 from that number. A player or enemy may be caught 'surprised' even if other members of their party are not. If you’re surprised, you can’t move or take an Action on your first turn of the combat, and you can’t take a Reaction until that turn ends.

**Surprise vs Hidden**

A creature is considered surprised only if they fail to spot every single enemy from the ambushing party. If they are not surprised, but still failed to perceive some enemies, those enemies are considered hidden.

- Eg. Joe is getting ambushed by 3 bugbears. Joe rolled a 15 on his perception. Bugbears 1 and 2 rolled a 20 on their stealth, but bugbear 3 only rolled a 5. Joe is not surprised because he spotted one of the bugbears. Bugbears 1 and 2 are hidden and reveal their position after making an attack. Bugbear 3 is in plain view.

**Sneaking Past Encounters**

Should your entire party's stealth beat the perception of the enemies, you can effectively "bypass" the encounter by sneaking past it. You may either move on to the next encounter in the area, or progress forwards to the next location. You do not gain any rewards for sneaking past an encounter.

## Casting Area of Effect Spells

Please keep in mind the distance of your companions when casting AOE spells. Know that if you are in the path of an AOE, you will be hit by it as well. There is some leeway with this, but try your best to use common sense when you are not using a map.

- I.E. The wizard casts Blazing Hands on a group of enemies. Enemy A is at 15ft, Enemy B is at 5ft. Unfortunately the Fighter was positioned at 10ft, and there is no way to avoid hitting them as well. Therefore, the Wizard will roll their AOE dice to see how many ENEMIES they hit, and then include the Fighter in the targeting of the spell as well.

## Cover & Optional Combat Actions

In general, keep this in mind: Whatever you can do, so can the enemy.

### Cover & Optional Combat Actions

The DMG’s optional action options may **not** be used on this server.

**Cover**

Theatre of Mind makes utilizing cover a very grey area. Use common sense as to whether or not there is sufficient cover to use, i.e. The desert likely has few if any options for cover. Additionally, if a player is allowed to just…take cover, then so can the enemies. Cover, unless it is total cover, does not prevent enemies from coming up beside you or from other angles.

This server does **not** allow anyone to use allies or enemies as cover.

## Looting Encounters

**Looting Standard Encounters**

Using the **!calc** function players can manually enter all party members (companions not included) and monster CRs to calculate the gold per character. XP is not earned through combat, and gold must be manually added to one's inventory using !coins command. Use the “Adjusted per PC” gold amount from the !calc function. You only earn coin for monsters you have redacted to 0 hp. If you flee, you cannot loot any gold, regardless of whether or not you defeated some of the monsters.

**!calc** **Function**

**!calc <player lvls> | <cr> <cr>**

- Eg. 2 level 5 players and 1 level 6 player defeated 3 CR 1 creatures and 1 CR 5 creature. The lootgen command would be:
- **!calc 5 5 6 | 3*1 5**

**!lootgen** **and Hoards**

Hoards are special encounters that award successful characters with magic items on top of the gold they earn from the **!calc** function.

Hoards will be identified as such in the pinned posts, instructing a player of the intended tier challenge and to run the **!lootgen** command. Only one player can roll for the loot. Run the command **!lootgen <hoard tier #>**. All players will roll **1d100** to determine order and will start with the player who rolled the highest number. On their turn, a player can select one item from the loot table. Players can choose to sell loot and divide the amount equally among them.

## Mining - what gives advantage and what doesn't on !mine checks

Familiars, mounts, and animal companions in general **cannot give the help action** while using the !mine command.

Why is that? Well because it's a little silly. But mostly because mining is very high value.

Instead, Proficiency with **Jeweler's Tools, Masonry Tools**, and **Smith's Tools** gives advantage to all !mine checks. You can also receive help from other players (giving up their chance to mine/hunt/forage/fish) or by Humanoid like companions (Simulacrums, Golems, etc).

## Encounter Conduct

When you volunteer to run peer combat, you are taking on the role as the DM. Please familiarize yourself with the [Avrae Commands](https://avrae.io/commands).

You can pull up a monster's full statblock by direct messaging the Avrae Bot with the command !monster "<monster name>". Homebrew monster will have a link to their statblock on CritterDB.  
  
Every player has a different way they enjoy to run encounters and different understanding of the rules. This is normal. Rely on respect and good communication skills to solve the issue. When in doubt, contact a Moderator. The Moderator serves as the ultimate GM. What they rule is the final rule on the subject. A subject can be revisited after the encounter to debrief and modify the ruling to ensure a positive play experience.

**Meta Knowledge**

Do your best to avoid using meta-knowledge about monsters, spells or locations. Would you character reasonably know a monsters AC, spells, resistances and vulnerabilities?

If there is a lack of clarity between the player and the DM, sort things out using communication. The DM should ask a player how they have that knowledge, and the player can provide a reasonable explanation. Any disputes can be settled by pinging a moderator.

**Confusion About Encounter Specifics**

Combat and encounters are messy, especially with theatre of mind, and miscommunication or different visualizations of the scene at hand happen all the time, especially when roleplaying character's actions as apposed to plainly listing them out.

Players can at any time request a DM clarify locations of enemies and allies, and DMs must provide accurate information to the best of their ability. If a player believes the DM misunderstood one of their actions or movements, the DM should avoid taking this personally. Mistakes happen, on all sides. The key is to handle these issues with respect. The inverse also applies if a player has made a mistake and forgotten to specify where their character was headed or what they're doing.

This is only a game, and retcons happen all the time. A simple discussion and adjustment is all that is required.

**Good Faith and Fairness**

DMing should be done in good faith. This means DMs should not intentionally ignore a player's questions for clarification, a player's action, or dismiss a player's concerns.

**Abuse & Issues**

Any player at any time can raise issue with the DMing conduct of any other player. The may do so privately with a Moderator who will then investigate the complaint.

Players may have their DMing privileges revoked if found to be abusive or unsuitable to fill the role.

## Encounter Editing

"Have you ever rolled an encounter that you wish there was more to? Well, worry no longer! With encounter editing, you'll be able to make those wishes become reality! No longer shall your storytelling ideas be constrained by the would be tyrant known as 'Avrae' and her accomplice, 'RNG'. Vote for Encounter editing today!"

The idea behind this is to allow for DMs (whoever is running encs at the time) to edit encounters to make them more difficult, within reason. Roll a large group of bandits and want more spice? Maybe toss another captain in there, and a veteran to serve as the real leader. Roll a bunch of zombies? Maybe toss in a zombie clot to represent the effect of a true horde, or even the necromancer that raised them. Just little things to add more spice to encounters when it feels appropriate.

Disclaimers:

- This would only be done with the full consent of the entire party.
- This could never be done to make an encounter easier than it was before and is only to be used to add more challenge or ✨ flair ✨
- The ability to do this would be a privilege that one could lose if used improperly, at the moderator's discretion

## Abandoning Players

**Abandoning Other Players**

To avoid OOC conflict, you must make it clear to the other players that your character may abandon the party should things "go south" **before** going on an adventure with those other characters. In character, you may lie, but please make it clear to the players that your character will not stay and fight if the encounter goes poorly. This is exclusively to avoid OOC conflict or resentment.

This rule does not apply in circumstances where one character is behaving recklessly and/or causing fights the party doesn't want to be involved in. I.e. A good aligned character wishes to leave a party/split where the characters are killing good aligned monsters.

## Additional Combat Rulings

**Potions in Combat**

Potions are consumable items. Drinking a potion or administering it to another creature requires a Bonus Action. Applying an oil might take longer as specified in its description. Once used, a potion takes effect immediately, and it is used up.

- We are updating to the 2024 Potions ruling. Please not that it specifically requires a Bonus Action - so no, you cannot double fist potions with your Action as well.
